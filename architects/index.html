What an architect does

An architect turns an idea for a building into drawings a builder can price and a permit office can approve. The work usually covers the layout, the look of the building, the structural and code requirements that apply, and the detailed construction documents a contractor builds from. Many architects also stay involved during construction, answering questions from the builder and checking that the work follows the drawings.

People hire an architect for custom homes, additions, remodels that move walls or change a roofline, and commercial spaces such as offices, restaurants, and shops. Small projects sometimes need only drawings for a permit. Larger projects need the full sequence, from first sketches to the final walkthrough.

Architect or architectural designer: what is the difference

A licensed architect has met a state's education, experience, and exam requirements. Only a licensed architect can stamp and seal drawings where a stamp is required, and the title architect is protected by state law. The rules differ by state.

An architectural designer is not a licensed architect, and cannot stamp or seal drawings. What a designer can do is design and draw projects that do not require an architect's stamp. In many states that includes most single-family homes, and many designers have years of residential experience and deep local knowledge. A permit office can still require stamped drawings in specific cases, and a structural engineer may need to sign off on parts of the design.

The practical question is what your project and your permit office require. Before you hire anyone, ask whether your project needs stamped drawings, and ask the person in front of you whether they can provide them or work with an engineer who can. Listings on this page keep the two titles separate so you can see which one you are calling.

How hiring an architect works

  1. Define the project. Write down what you want to build, your rough budget for construction, and your timeline. Firms can give you a useful first answer with just that.
  2. Talk to two or three firms. Most offer an initial consultation. Look at their past work and ask about projects like yours, in size and type.
  3. Get the proposal in writing. It should name the scope, the phases included, the fee basis, what is not included, and how changes are handled.
  4. Design phases. Typical stages are schematic design (rough layouts and the overall look), design development (materials and systems get specific), and construction documents (the detailed drawings used for permits and building).
  5. Permits. Your firm prepares the drawings your city or county needs. Review times vary a lot by place.
  6. Building. Many firms offer construction administration: site visits, builder questions, and review of the work against the drawings.

What an architect costs

There is no standard price, and any firm that quotes a firm total before seeing your project is guessing. Fees are usually structured one of three ways:

  • A percentage of the construction cost. Common on full-service custom homes and commercial work, because the design work grows with the size of the build.
  • A flat fee for a defined scope. Common for smaller projects and drawing-only work, such as an addition or a permit set.
  • An hourly rate. Common for consultations, feasibility questions, and early-stage advice.

What you pay depends on the size of the project, how finished the design needs to be, whether engineering is included, and your local market. A drawing-only set for a simple addition costs a fraction of full service on a custom home. A short call gets you further than any guide: describe the project and ask each firm for its fee basis, in writing, before you sign.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Are you a licensed architect, or an architectural designer? (Either can be the right hire. You should know which you are getting.)
  • Does my project need stamped drawings, and can you provide them?
  • Have you done projects like mine, nearby? Can I see examples?
  • Who will actually do the design work, and who is my contact?
  • What is the fee basis, and what is not included?
  • How long do the design phases usually take for a project this size?
  • How do revisions work if I want changes after a phase is approved?
  • Will you coordinate with my builder, or help me choose one?
  • What do you need from me to start?
