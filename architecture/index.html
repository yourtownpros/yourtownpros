How YourTownPros works under the hood

YourTownPros lists more than 14,000 local businesses across all 50 states and Puerto Rico, with figures as of November 2026. Every listing page passed a series of checks before it published, and the checking does not stop at publish. This page explains how, in plain language first and in engineering detail second.

Where a listing comes from

Real businesses only. We build the directory town by town from public sources: the business's own website, its public profiles, and public records. Nothing about a listing is invented. If we cannot confirm a business exists, it does not get a page. Listing itself is always free, and a listing is not an endorsement. Some businesses pay for Featured placement, which is always labeled Sponsored; it never changes a rating, a review count, or the order of the free listings.

Checked before it publishes

Facts are checked by rules, not by opinion. Before a listing goes live, its details have to pass a fixed set of automated validations: phone numbers and addresses are format-checked, duplicates are caught by matching on phone number and business name, and closed businesses are screened out. Hours are copied only from the business's own website or its public Google profile, and we record which source they came from. Ratings are always shown with the source named next to the score, so you can check a rating against its source yourself instead of taking our word for it. Written content for visitors, like the guides on category pages, is read by three independent reviewers who see only the page itself, with no background and no history, before it is allowed to ship.

Fast on a phone

Most people find us on a phone, often on a slow connection. So listing pages are not giant prebuilt files. They are assembled at the edge, at a server close to the visitor, from a compact database, and each page is kept deliberately small. That is how the site can hold tens of thousands of pages and still load quickly.

Kept current

Businesses open, close, move, and change their hours. Automated verification loops keep re-checking listings on a continuing cycle after they publish, and when a fact stops checking out, the listing is corrected. Owners can also claim their listing free and request changes; every owner change is reviewed by a person before it goes live. Spotted something wrong? Tell us through the contact page and we will check it against the source.

What to do next

Looking for a pro? Start with the search on the home page. Own a business that is listed here? Claim your listing, free, to request corrections and updates.

The system design

For the technically curious, here is the same pipeline in engineering terms. In one sentence: data is staged, locked against double-processing, checked by deterministic rules, read by reviewers, stored in a versioned database at the edge, and rendered near the visitor. If the plain-language version above told you what you needed, you can stop here.

  • Staged ingestion: new data always lands in a staging area first. Nothing goes straight to the live site.
  • Atomic claims lock: work moves through the pipeline in batches, and each batch is claimed exactly once before it is processed, so two runs can never write over each other.
  • Deterministic fact gates: the rule-based validations described above. Data that fails a gate is held back with the reason recorded, never silently dropped and never waved through.
  • 3-reviewer Goldfish gate: visitor-facing copy is read by three independent reviewers before it ships. We call it the Goldfish gate because each reviewer sees the page fresh, the way a first-time visitor does.
  • D1 + dataset_version edge cache: approved data lives in a database at the edge. Every change carries a version stamp, so rendered pages refresh in step with the data instead of going stale.
  • Edge rendering: when you open a listing, the page is assembled on a server near you from that database, which keeps it fast and small.
