Water Heater Replacement Cost in Denver (2026)

From YourTownPros: YourTownPros is a directory of local home service companies. It publishes these guides, using prices from the named sources below, so Denver homeowners can compare quotes before they call. YourTownPros does not do this work itself and does not endorse any business listed.

The short answer

Most Denver homeowners replacing a standard tank water heater with a similar model pay in the typical national range of $881 to $1,827 installed, with an average of $1,349. That range is Angi's, updated October 16, 2026 and based on 30,979 verified projects. Denver adds a small city permit fee, $35 at the valuation of a typical swap. Tankless and heat pump models cost more up front, and a heat pump water heater can come back down with an Xcel Energy rebate of up to $2,250 (a $750 standard rebate plus a $1,500 limited-time bonus that requires an invoice dated in 2026). One caution up front: heat pump pricing is the least settled number in this guide. Angi's national installed range is $1,200 to $3,500, while UniColorado, a Denver installer, quotes $6,900 to $8,000 installed in Denver before rebates, so budget from local quotes for that model, not from the national range. Every range on this page is a typical range from a named source, not a quote. Your price depends on the unit you pick, the fuel it uses, and how much extra work your house needs.

What drives the price up or down

Access is the quiet cost. A heater in an open basement swaps fast. A heater up stairs, in a tight closet, or in a spot the new unit barely fits takes longer, and Angi notes that relocating a water heater entirely can add $150 to $3,400. Venting changes add up on gas models: a power vent system adds $300 to $600 to the unit price, plus $300 to $500 for the electrical work it needs. A bigger tank costs more than a smaller one, which is why the 75 gallon row in Angi's size table runs $1,100 to $4,000 against $800 to $2,300 for a 40 gallon. The largest jump is a tankless conversion, because the new unit rarely lands where the old tank sat: conversion labor alone runs $150 to $2,500 in Angi's data, a new gas line averages $260 to $820, and an electric tankless model can require a service upgrade to 200 amps. Smaller add-ons matter too. An expansion tank runs $90 to $350, and removing and hauling away the old heater runs $100 to $500.

Repair or replace?

Repair when the tank itself is sound and the fault is a part: Angi puts tank water heater repairs at $100 to $500. Replace when water is leaking from the tank itself, and plan on replacement as the unit passes about ten years, the average lifespan Angi reports. For the full decision walkthrough, see the homeowner answer hub guide: Water Heater Trouble: Repair It or Replace It?

Denver specifics: permit, inspection, and rebates

A permit is required. On the City and County of Denver's own permitting page, new or replacement water heaters, including solar, at a single-family house or duplex need no drawings, go through the Quick review process, and require a trades permit. Quick permits are issued without plan review, and compliance is checked by inspection instead. Denver prices permits on a valuation table (fee schedule ADMIN 138, revised May 21, 2024): $35 for work valued at $501 to $2,000, which covers a typical like-for-like swap, then $8 for each additional $1,000 of value. Your installer normally pulls the permit. A bid that quietly leaves the permit off is worth a direct question before you sign.

Rebates change the math on one model only. Xcel Energy pays a heat pump water heater rebate to its residential electric, gas, or combination customers in Colorado: a $750 standard rebate plus a limited-time bonus rebate of $1,500, for up to $2,250 back in total. The equipment must be an ENERGY STAR rated heat pump water heater with a minimum 3.3 UEF, listed on the AHRI Certified Directory, new and permanently installed at the address on the claim, and must not have received a rebate from another program; units with storage over 80 gallons are not eligible, and indirect and commercial units are excluded. Xcel states no purchase-date requirement for the standard rebate. The bonus requires an invoice dated in 2026, and its funding is limited and first-come, first-served until the Public Utility Commission approved funding is expended. Apply by September 30 of the year after the equipment was installed, and there is no limit on the number of units or the rebate amount per customer. Colorado also offers a state heat pump tax credit of up to $250, with at least $83 of it reaching the homeowner, claimed through a registered contractor. The federal energy efficient home improvement credit for heat pump water heaters ended for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025, so do not budget for it on a 2026 install.

Run your own numbers with the free Xcel water heater rebate calculator: it applies the same $750 standard rebate and $1,500 limited-time bonus to the answers you give, and shows the reasoning behind the number it gives you.

How to get a quote you can compare

Your next step: pick two or three companies, from the Denver plumbers list at the end of this guide or your own search, and ask each of them the same three questions. First, are you licensed for this work, and what is the license number? Second, will you give written pricing that spells out the unit, the labor, the permit fees, and disposal of the old heater? Third, what warranty covers the parts and the labor, and is it in writing? A quote that lists only one lump sum, or that pressures you to decide the same day, is a reason to get a second quote. When two companies answer the same three questions in writing, the quotes can finally be compared line by line.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to replace a water heater in Denver?

A typical like-for-like tank replacement runs $881 to $1,827 installed, average $1,349, in Angi's 2026 national data. Denver's permit adds $35 at a typical job value. Tankless runs $1,400 to $3,900 installed, and heat pump models run higher before rebates.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Denver?

Yes. The City and County of Denver lists new or replacement water heaters as Quick permit work for single-family and duplex homes: no drawings, no plan review, a trades permit, and an inspection to check the finished work.

Is there a rebate for a heat pump water heater in Denver?

Yes, if you are an Xcel Energy residential electric, gas, or combination customer in Colorado. Xcel pays a $750 standard rebate on a qualifying heat pump water heater, plus a limited-time bonus rebate of $1,500, for up to $2,250 back. The unit must be ENERGY STAR rated with a minimum 3.3 UEF and listed on the AHRI Certified Directory, and the bonus requires an invoice dated in 2026. The federal tax credit for these units ended on December 31, 2025.

Should I repair my water heater or replace it?

Repair it if the tank is sound and the problem is a replaceable part; Angi puts tank repairs at $100 to $500. Replace it if the tank itself is leaking, or if the unit is past about ten years old and repairs are adding up.
