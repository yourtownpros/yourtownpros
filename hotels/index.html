The kinds of places to stay, and what the words mean

The lodging words get used loosely, and the differences are real when you are the one sleeping there. Short versions:

  • Hotel: staffed, front desk, rooms by the night, a dozen rooms to several hundred.
  • Motel: built for drivers. Rooms open toward the parking area, check-in is fast, and the good independent ones are some of the best value in American travel.
  • Inn: smaller, often historic, a host or small staff, a more personal stay. Many include breakfast; ask, because not all do.
  • Bed and breakfast: a home or small property, host on site or nearby, breakfast included. Rooms are few, bathrooms may be shared in older houses, and house rules are part of the deal.
  • Lodge: tied to a place, a park, a lake, a ski area, with shared common space and activities nearby.
  • Resort: the property is itself the destination, pools and dining on site. Ask what the resort fee covers before you book, because it is rarely optional.
  • Hostel: dorm beds and private rooms at budget prices, shared kitchens and common rooms.

How to choose where to stay

  1. Start with the map, not the star rating. In a beach town, the difference between oceanfront and one block back is the difference between watching the water from your balcony and walking to it. In a mountain town, distance to the park entrance or the ski lift decides your mornings. Listings here show the address, so you can see where a property actually sits.
  2. Decide what kind of stay fits the trip. A motel for a road night, an inn or B&B for a weekend you want to remember, a resort when the property is the plan, a hotel when you want a front desk at any hour.
  3. Read the rating with its source. A rating only means something if you know where it came from, so every rating on this site names its source next to it. A small inn with a 4.9 from 40 reviews and a highway hotel with a 4.2 from 900 reviews are both telling you something true; they are not telling you the same thing. Low ratings stay on the list on purpose: a 2.6 is information too, and hiding it would turn this page into an ad.
  4. Check the season. Tourist towns swing hard: the same room can cost three times as much in peak weeks, and some inns and lodges close for a shoulder season. If your dates are flexible, a week either way can change the price more than any discount site.
  5. Ask about the unglamorous things before you book: parking (free, charged, or a walk away), late check-in if you are arriving after dark, cancellation terms, and whether breakfast is included or next door.

Booking direct: how it works here

Each listing shows the property's phone number, and where the property publishes its own booking page, the listing points at it. Booking direct means booking through the route the business itself published: its own site or its front desk. We do not take bookings, we do not add fees, we do not hold rooms, and we earn nothing when you book. There is no commission in it for us.

Why book direct at all: the property can answer questions a booking site cannot (which rooms are quiet, whether the crib fits, whether the lot takes a trailer), changes and cancellations go through one desk instead of two companies, and small properties keep the commission they would have paid a booking platform. If a property's own site sends you to a booking engine, that is still its published route; follow it.

What areas mean in a tourist town

Listing addresses cluster, and the clusters mean things. In beach towns: oceanfront (on the sand), ocean side (across the road from the sand), and bay or sound side (calmer water, sunsets, usually cheaper). Downtown and historic district properties trade beach or trail access for walkable restaurants. Highway and interstate properties trade charm for price, parking, and an easy exit in the morning. In mountain towns, in-town means walkable dinners and a drive to the trailhead; at the park entrance or the ski base means the reverse. The right answer depends on what your days look like, and the address on each listing is the fact to decide from.

Questions to ask before you book

  • What are check-in and check-out times, and what happens if I arrive late?
  • Is parking on site, and does it cost anything?
  • What is the cancellation policy, in days and dollars?
  • Is breakfast included? If not, what is nearby in the morning?
  • Are there resort, facility, or cleaning fees on top of the room rate?
  • Which rooms are the quiet ones, and can I request one?
  • Do you take dogs, and what does that cost?
  • Is the property seasonal, and are all amenities open on my dates?
