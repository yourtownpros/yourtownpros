Questions, answered plainly

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Denver?

Yes. Denver's permit guidance lists new or replacement water heaters, including solar, as work that requires a trades permit. For a house or duplex it goes through the city's quick permit process, with no drawings and no plan review.

Do I need a permit to replace a furnace in Denver?

Yes, in the usual case. Denver lists forced air heating systems, furnaces, as trades permit work. The city's exemption for like-unit repairs stops applying when the work disconnects and reconnects a gas line or a flue vent, which a furnace replacement does.

Do I need a permit to add an outlet in Denver?

Yes. Denver's guidance lists new lighting and receptacles, and new GFCI outlets, as electrical work that requires a trades permit through the quick permit process.

When can I skip the permit?

Denver exempts general repairs that do not alter existing systems, such as replacing like fixtures, and mechanical repairs of like units that do not disconnect a gas line, flue vent, refrigerant line, steam or hot water line, or boiler safety controls. If your job is not listed on the city's page, check with the city before you start.

Does this checker cover other cities?

No. It follows the City and County of Denver's published guidance for single-family houses and duplexes. Other cities in the metro area run their own permit rules.
