How to choose a web designer or marketing agency

For the person hiring

YourTownPros is a free directory of local businesses, and this page lists web design and marketing firms by city. Today it lists 13 firms across 5 states. Hiring someone to build or market your website is a decision most business owners make once and then live with for years, so it pays to slow down at the choosing stage. Three checks settle most of it before price even comes up.

First, look at work like yours. Ask to see live sites the firm built for businesses of your size, and open them on your phone. A portfolio that only shows screenshots is telling you less than a list of working sites you can visit, click, and call from.

Second, ask who owns what. Your domain name, your website files, your analytics, and your ad accounts should be registered in your name, with logins you hold. If a firm cannot say plainly how you would take your site with you if you ever left, that is the answer to how the relationship works.

Third, get the quote in writing, itemized. Design, build, content, hosting, and ongoing changes are separate things with separate costs. A single lump sum hides which of them you are actually buying, and it makes the next firm's quote impossible to compare fairly.

Every firm on this page is listed with the name, phone number, and city it publishes, so you can run the three checks above against a real business before you call. Pick your city from the list and call the firm.

WordPress, a builder, or a custom site: which fits your business

This page is from YourTownPros, a directory of local businesses. The website design firms are listed above this guide, each with its name, phone number, and address, so once you know which kind of site you need, your next step is to pick a firm from those listings and call it.

Most small business websites are built one of three ways, and the right choice depends on what the site has to do, not on which option sounds most advanced.

A builder platform (Squarespace, Wix, and similar) is usually the fastest and lowest cost way to get a clean site live. You edit pages yourself in a visual editor, hosting and security are handled for you, and the monthly cost is predictable. The trade-off is the ceiling: unusual layouts, custom booking flows, and features the platform does not offer are hard or impossible to add, and moving the site elsewhere later usually means rebuilding it.

WordPress sits in the middle. It can run anything from a five-page brochure site to a full store, plugins exist for most features a local business needs, and because WordPress is widely used, another developer can usually take over the site later, though a poorly built one may need repair work first. The trade-off is upkeep: updates, backups, and security are someone's job, either yours, your host's, or a developer you pay monthly.

A custom built site is code written for your business alone. It fits when your site has to do something specific that platforms and plugins do not cover, for example a custom quoting tool or a customer portal tied to your own systems. It costs the most to build and to change, and you depend on whoever wrote the code, so it earns its price only when the specific need is real.

A practical way to decide: list the three things your site must do in its first year. If a builder covers all three, start there. If one of them needs a plugin or an integration, WordPress is usually the honest fit. If one of them does not exist yet in any platform, that is the custom conversation, and it should start with that feature, not with a full redesign. The firms listed above work across these options. Several of their listing pages walk through one of these comparisons in detail, in plain terms, so when you call a firm from the listings above, you arrive at that first call already knowing which questions matter for your business.
